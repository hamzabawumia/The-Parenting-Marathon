## 🌱 Overview

The period from **12 to 24 months** is one of the most dramatic transitions in early childhood.

Your baby is becoming a toddler.

During this period, children become increasingly mobile, curious, expressive and independent. Walking, exploring, scribbling, playing, communicating and imitating adults become major parts of everyday life.

It is also the period when parents discover that a child who previously stayed relatively still can now reach, climb, grab, open, pull, draw and investigate almost everything.

The parenting strategy therefore begins to change.

Instead of simply protecting the baby from the environment, parents increasingly need to **prepare the environment for an active toddler**.

---

## 🎯 Goals for This Age

The goal is **not** to produce an unusually advanced child.

The goal is to provide a safe, stimulating environment in which normal development can flourish.

### Developmental Goals

* Encourage walking and physical activity.
* Encourage safe exploration.
* Support early communication and vocabulary.
* Read regularly.
* Encourage scribbling, colouring and creative activities.
* Introduce simple routines.
* Encourage interaction with other children.
* Develop independence gradually.
* Teach simple boundaries.
* Encourage the child to help tidy toys.
* Provide opportunities for outdoor play.

### Parenting Goals

* Make the home toddler-safe.
* Establish predictable routines.
* Spend time talking to the child.
* Read together regularly.
* Give the child opportunities to make simple choices.
* Encourage active play.
* Introduce toilet training when the child shows signs of readiness.
* Observe the child's individual personality and preferences.
* Avoid relying on screens as the primary source of entertainment.

## 🧠 Development Focus

### Cognitive Growth

Your child is becoming increasingly interested in cause and effect.

They may:

* Explore objects by touching, moving and manipulating them.
* Imitate adults.
* Become interested in books and pictures.
* Begin experimenting with drawing and scribbling.
* Recognize familiar people and objects.
* Develop preferences for particular toys, songs and programmes.
* Follow increasingly simple instructions.

### Emotional Development

The child is beginning to develop a stronger sense of independence.

You may hear more:

> "No!"

or encounter a child who wants to do something independently even when they are not yet capable of doing it.

Frustration is normal at this stage because the child's desire to communicate and act independently is developing faster than their ability to do so.

### Social Development

Children increasingly enjoy interaction with adults and other children.

Starting a new environment such as creche can produce temporary behavioural changes while the child adjusts to:

* New people
* New routines
* New surroundings
* Separation from parents
* New expectations

## ❤️ Parenting Tips

### 1. Give the Child Places Where They Are Allowed to Explore

Toddlers are going to explore.

The question is whether they will explore **your carefully arranged living room** or an environment you have prepared for them.

Create safe areas where the child can:

* Play
* Read
* Colour
* Make noise
* Move around
* Experiment

The more appropriate opportunities you provide, the less often you need to say:

> **"No!"**

---

### 2. Give Them a Place to Scribble

From around the first year onward, expect scribbling to become increasingly interesting.

And once the child discovers crayons, the walls may suddenly become extremely attractive.

Rather than simply prohibiting scribbling, create a designated scribbling area.

You can use:

* A large writing board
* A chalkboard
* A whiteboard
* Large sheets of paper
* Cardboard attached securely to a wall

One inexpensive solution is to attach a large piece of brown packaging cardboard to a section of wall.

Teach the child:

> **"This is your drawing board."**

Whenever they start drawing elsewhere, calmly redirect them to their designated area.

The objective is not merely to stop the behaviour. It is to **teach the child where the behaviour is appropriate**.

---

### 3. Start Giving the Child Their Own Space

Around 18 months, consider getting a small table and chair.

In our experience, this was surprisingly successful.

The child had a place of his own for:

* Eating
* Colouring
* Looking at books
* Playing
* Quiet activities

A small child-sized environment can encourage independence and make everyday activities easier.

---

### 4. Establish Simple Routines

Toddlers benefit from predictable routines.

For example:

**Wake → breakfast → play → lunch → nap → play → dinner → quiet activities → bedtime**

The exact schedule will vary from child to child.

The important thing is consistency.

---

### 5. Give Simple Choices

As independence develops, allow the child to make small choices.

For example:

* "Do you want the red shirt or the blue shirt?"
* "Do you want the book or the blocks?"
* "Do you want to sit here or there?"

Giving limited choices can help satisfy the child's growing desire for independence while keeping the parent in control of the overall situation.

## 🎨 Activities & Learning

### Colouring

Colouring can become an excellent activity during this period.

At around 18 months, our child started receiving colouring activities at creche.

Before long, colouring became one of his favourite pastimes.

He would regularly want plain paper and something to colour in the evening.

This eventually became part of our evening routine.

Colouring provides opportunities to develop:

* Hand control
* Fine motor skills
* Concentration
* Creativity
* Recognition of colours
* Early mark-making skills

Don't worry about whether the child stays inside the lines.

At this age, the scribbling itself is part of the learning process.

### Reading

Read to your child regularly.

Books do not need to be complicated.

Picture books featuring:

* Animals
* People
* Vehicles
* Colours
* Objects
* Simple stories

are excellent opportunities for interaction.

Don't merely read the words.

Point at pictures.

Ask questions.

Name objects.

Make animal sounds.

Let the child turn the pages.

The interaction is part of the learning.

### Singing

Children's songs can be excellent entertainment and learning tools.

Sing together rather than relying exclusively on recordings.

Songs can introduce:

* Words
* Sounds
* Numbers
* Letters
* Rhythm
* Repetition

### Outdoor Play

Toddlers need opportunities to move.

Whenever practical, provide opportunities for:

* Walking
* Running
* Climbing safely
* Pushing and pulling toys
* Playing outdoors
* Exploring different environments

Physical activity is not merely entertainment. It is an important part of development.

## 🧸 Toys & Equipment

You do not need hundreds of toys.

A simple collection of appropriate toys can provide plenty of opportunities for exploration.

### Recommended Basics

* Toy box or bucket
* Small drum
* Rattle
* Toy telephone
* Children's books
* Colouring materials
* Paper
* Simple musical toys
* Age-appropriate ride-on toy
* Small table and chair

### Toy Storage

Get a toy box or bucket and begin teaching the child that toys have a home.

At first, the parent will do most of the tidying.

But the habit can begin surprisingly early:

> **Play → finish → put toys away.**

Make cleanup part of the routine rather than treating it as a punishment.

## 📺 Music, Television & Screens

Children at this age can become fascinated by music and moving images.

In our own experience, children's songs could hold our child's attention for long periods.

Later, around 18 months, he became particularly interested in children's programming and eventually developed a strong preference for *SpongeBob SquarePants*.

Other programmes were offered but did not interest him nearly as much.

This illustrates an important point:

> **Children develop their own preferences.**

However, a parent's experience with screens should not automatically be interpreted as a recommendation for extended screen use.

For young toddlers, screens should not replace:

* Sleep
* Physical activity
* Outdoor play
* Reading
* Conversation
* Social interaction
* Hands-on exploration

When screens are used, parents can make the experience more useful by watching with the child when possible, talking about what is happening and choosing age-appropriate content.

A screen can occupy a child.

**Interaction with a parent develops a child.**

## 🚽 Toilet Training

Toilet training can be tempting to start early.

We tried it during this period.

It failed spectacularly.

Eventually, we decided to try again when the child was older.

The important lesson is that toilet training is not a competition.

Children become ready at different times.

Look for signs of readiness rather than focusing exclusively on age.

Possible signs include:

* Showing awareness of urination or bowel movements
* Staying dry for longer periods
* Showing interest in the toilet
* Following simple instructions
* Communicating their needs
* Being able to help with clothing

Introduce the idea gradually and avoid turning toilet training into a battle.

If the child is clearly not ready, it is perfectly reasonable to pause and try again later.

## 🛁 Bathing & Personal Care

Once a child can stand confidently, parents may find it easier to transition from baby bathing arrangements to bathing in the bathroom.

However, **standing does not mean the child is safe around water.**

### Bathing Safety

Always:

* Supervise the child actively.
* Stay within arm's reach.
* Use a suitable non-slip bathing surface.
* Prepare everything before starting.
* Keep dangerous objects out of reach.
* Never leave the child alone in or near water.

A bath mat or bathing aid is not a substitute for adult supervision.

> **A toddler in water requires an attentive adult.**

## 🏫 Creche & Social Development

Around 18 months, our child started creche.

We noticed some behavioural changes during the transition.

There was:

* More crying
* Greater interest in playing outdoors
* A change in routine
* An adjustment period

Eventually, however, he adapted.

Starting creche is a significant change for a young child.

Parents should expect an adjustment period and provide reassurance, consistency and patience.

At the same time, persistent or severe behavioural changes should not simply be dismissed as "adjustment." If something concerns you, discuss it with the child's healthcare professional.

### Schoolwork Can Become a Routine

Our child's main homework at this stage was colouring on paper.

This soon became a favourite evening activity.

A simple routine developed around **7:30–8:30 PM**, when he would sit down with paper and colouring materials.

The lesson is simple:

> **Children can develop routines around activities they genuinely enjoy.**

## ⚠️ Challenges to Expect

### Scribbling Everywhere

Your toddler may decide that walls, furniture and floors are excellent drawing surfaces.

**Solution:** Give them a designated drawing area and consistently redirect them.

### Increasing Independence

Your child may want to do things without help.

This is healthy, but it can also create frustration.

**Solution:** Give them safe opportunities to make simple choices.

### Tantrums and Frustration

The child's desires are developing faster than their communication skills.

**Solution:** Stay calm, acknowledge the emotion and maintain simple boundaries.

### Creche Adjustment

Starting creche may temporarily increase crying or behavioural changes.

**Solution:** Maintain consistency and give the child time to adjust.

### Constant Movement

Once walking and exploring become established, the child can become difficult to contain.

**Solution:** Childproof the environment rather than trying to prevent exploration.

### Toilet Training

The child may simply not be ready.

**Solution:** Don't turn it into a battle. Pause and try again later.

## 💡 Lessons From Our Experience

These are some of the things we learned during the 12–24 month period.

### Give Them Somewhere to Scribble

If you don't provide a legal place for the crayons to go, the child may create one.

A large piece of cardboard attached to the wall can be surprisingly effective.

### Simple Toys Can Be Enough

A drum, a rattle, a toy phone and some books can provide enormous entertainment.

You don't necessarily need expensive electronic toys.

### Children Have Preferences

You may carefully select one programme or toy while the child becomes fascinated by something completely different.

Our child enjoyed *SpongeBob SquarePants* but showed little interest in some of the other programmes we tried.

### Cardboard Is Surprisingly Valuable

Large pieces of cardboard can become drawing surfaces and creative materials.

Sometimes the cheapest solution is one of the most useful.

### A Small Table and Chair Can Be Transformative

Having their own little space can make children surprisingly happy.

It can become a place for eating, colouring, reading and playing.

### Don't Compare Children Unnecessarily

One child may love a tricycle.

Another may prefer a ride-on car.

One child may love books.

Another may want to spend the entire day outside.

Individual differences are normal.

### Failed Attempts Are Still Useful

Our first attempt at toilet training failed.

That did not make the attempt worthless.

It taught us that our child was probably not ready.

Parenting involves experimentation.

You try something.

You observe.

You adjust.

Then you try again.

## ❤️ Final Thoughts

The period between one and two years can be exhausting, chaotic and wonderfully entertaining.

Your child is changing almost every day.

One day they are learning to walk.

The next day they are trying to draw on your wall.

Then they discover books.

Then colouring.

Then a favourite song.

Then a favourite cartoon.

Then they suddenly want to do everything themselves.

It can feel like chaos.

But beneath that chaos, something remarkable is happening.

**Your baby is becoming a person with preferences, abilities, curiosity and independence.**

Your job is not to control every aspect of that process.

Your job is to provide:

**Safety.**

**Love.**

**Boundaries.**

**Opportunities to explore.**

**And plenty of patience.**

They will not be this small forever.

Enjoy them while they are.
